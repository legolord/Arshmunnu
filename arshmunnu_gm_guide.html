GM Philosophy

Arshmunnu is not a simulation to be beaten. It is a world to be inhabited. Your job as Game Master is threefold:

  1. Portray a living world. Arshmunnu breathes — empires rise and fall, seasons turn, factions scheme. Show the players a world that exists beyond their characters.
  2. Be a fair arbiter. The dice are your ally, not your weapon. Roll in the open. Let consequences land where they fall.
  3. Make every session memorable. Whether through horror, wonder, politics, or combat — give them something to talk about.

The GM Agenda

The Five Principles

1. Consequence is King
Every meaningful action produces a meaningful reaction. If the players burn a bridge, make sure they hear the timber crack for sessions to come.
2. Information is Power
Never hide plot-critical information behind a single roll. If they search the room, they find clues. Rolls determine quality of information, not existence.
3. The World Does Not Wait
If the players ignore the cult summoning, the cult succeeds. Factions have their own clocks. The world moves regardless of PC attention.
4. Describe, Don't Dictate
Tell them what they see, hear, and smell. Let them decide what it means. Never tell a player how their character feels — show them why they might feel it.
5. Failure is Interesting
A failed roll is never "nothing happens." It is "something different happens." The lockpick breaks. The guard arrives. The informant lies.

Session Prep

The 30-Minute Prep Method

  1. Review last session (5 min) — What happened? What threads are loose?
  2. Advance faction clocks (5 min) — What did NPCs accomplish?
  3. Prepare 3 scenes (10 min) — A combat, a social, and a mystery/exploration scene
  4. Prepare 2 complications (5 min) — What could go wrong in each scene?
  5. Prepare 1 hook (5 min) — What pulls them toward next session?

ExplorationTest preparation and perceptionWhat is hidden here, and what is watching?
MysteryTest deduction and patienceWhat really happened, and who benefits from the lie?

Role Composition

A good encounter needs roles. Aim for:

  • 1-2 Frontliners (line/brute — hold position, absorb damage)
  • 1-2 Damage dealers (striker/artillery — kill things fast)
  • 0-1 Controller (manipulate battlefield, disable PCs)
  • 0-1 Support/Leader (buff allies, heal, command)

Scene Clocks

Clocks are progress trackers that build tension. Divide a circle into segments (usually 4-8). When conditions are met, fill a segment. When full, something happens.

Types of Clocks

Tension Clock
6 segments. Fills when: players take loud actions, fail stealth, or waste time. When full: reinforcements arrive, the alarm sounds, or the ritual completes.
Escape Clock
4 segments. Fills when: the villain takes damage, uses an ability, or a round passes. When full: the villain escapes, seals the exit, or transforms.
Discovery Clock
8 segments. Fills when: players investigate, find clues, or talk to NPCs. When full: the truth is revealed — for better or worse.
Faction Clock
6 segments. Fills between sessions. When full: the faction accomplishes a major goal. Use multiple faction clocks to show the world moving.

Morale System

Not every creature fights to the death. Morale makes combat more realistic and gives players tactical options beyond "kill everything."

Morale Check

Enemies test morale when:

  • Their leader falls
  • They lose half their number
  • A particularly frightening PC ability is used
  • An environmental hazard threatens them
  • They are offered a credible way out

Roll: d20 + leader's WIL modifier (or their own WIL if leaderless)

ResultOutcome
15+Hold firm
10-14Hesitate — lose next turn's first AP
5-9Retreat in good order — withdraw toward exit
Below 5Rout — drop weapons, flee chaotically

Breaking Morale

Players can deliberately try to break morale:

  • Intimidate: Bearing + WIL vs enemy WIL
  • Show of force: Drop the biggest enemy dramatically
  • Negotiate: Offer terms that are better than fighting
  • Environmental: Collapse a structure, start a fire, create chaos

Mass Combat

When armies clash, shift to unit-level resolution. PCs command, fight as heroes, or both.

Hero Actions in Mass Combat

Each round, a PC can take one Hero Action:

  • Lead the Charge: +2 to unit's next attack roll
  • Hold the Line: +2 to unit's DL until next turn
  • Assassinate: Attempt to kill an enemy commander
  • Rally: Restore a morale level to a shaken unit
  • Scout: Reveal enemy disposition and hidden units

Dungeon Building

The ARP Dungeon Method

A dungeon is not a series of rooms with monsters. It is a place with a purpose. Ask these questions before drawing a single room:

  1. Who built this? — The architecture tells a story
  2. Why was it built? — Purpose shapes layout and hazards
  3. Who lives here now? — Current inhabitants change everything
  4. What do they want? — Motivation drives encounter design
  5. What secret does it hold? — Every dungeon has something hidden

Trap Design

Every trap should have:

  • A tell — something observant players can notice
  • A trigger — what sets it off
  • An effect — what happens
  • A bypass — how clever players avoid it

Plot Structures

The Five Plot Engines

1. The Mystery
Hook: Something is wrong and no one knows why.
Structure: Clue → Red Herring → Revelation → Consequence
Example: A town's children are drawing the same symbol. No one taught it to them.
2. The Heist
Hook: Something valuable is protected by something dangerous.
Structure: Planning → Infiltration → Complication → Escape → Fallout
Example: Steal the Pontiff's private correspondence from the Rammalia vaults.
3. The Chase
Hook: Something is moving and must be caught or stopped.
Structure: Pursuit → Obstacle → Choice → Confrontation → Resolution
Example: A rogue mage carries a void-tome toward the Imperial capital.
4. The Siege
Hook: A place must be held against overwhelming force.
Structure: Preparation → Assault → Turning Point → Resolution
Example: Defend a border fortress against a Gothian host.
5. The Conspiracy
Hook: Powerful people are lying about something important.
Structure: Suspicion → Investigation → Danger → Truth → Reckoning
Example: The Emperor's heir is an imposter — and the real heir is still alive.

Death & Consequences

Making Death Matter

In Arshmunnu, death is not a revolving door. When a character dies:

  1. The body stays. Unless retrieved, it becomes part of the environment.
  2. Allies mourn. NPCs who knew the PC react — some seek vengeance, some lose hope.
  3. O debts remain. Unfinished business becomes plot hooks for the surviving party.
  4. Replacement characters enter at a cost. They start with fewer resources and must earn trust.

Consequences Beyond Death

Sometimes living is worse than dying:

  • Captivity: Imprisoned, interrogated, broken out later
  • Disfigurement: Visible scars affect social encounters
  • Corruption: Void-touched — gain power, lose humanity
  • Betrayal: Forced to choose between allies
  • Oath-breaking: Reputation shattered, standing reduced to 0

Void Corruption

The Void does not simply destroy — it changes. Those who traffic with void magic risk permanent transformation.

Corruption Track

Reducing Corruption

  • Purification ritual at a major temple (costly, dangerous)
  • Long rest in sanctified ground (reduces by 1 stage over a month)
  • Sacrifice of a valued possession (DM discretion)
  • Complete abstinence from void magic for an extended period

Troubleshooting

Common Problems & Solutions

"My players are too cautious"

Add time pressure — a clock, a threat, a consequence for delay. Make inaction expensive. If they still won't act, ask them directly: "What does your character want to do?"

"My players are too reckless"

Let them face the consequences of recklessness — not as punishment, but as story. Capture, not death. Debt, not destruction. Make them feel the cost of carelessness.

"One player dominates"

Address it in-game: their character becomes notorious, drawing attention. Address it out-of-game: "I want everyone to have a moment this session." Give spotlight questions to quieter players.

"Players killed my main villain early"

Congratulations — they just changed the story. The villain's death has consequences. Their organisation fractures. A worse villain rises. The power vacuum creates new problems. Adapt.

"I have no idea what happens next"

Ask the players. "What do you want to pursue?" Their answer is your next session. Alternatively: advance your faction clocks and see what happens when the world moves.

Combatants

Running the v2 Callings

The Player's Guide v2 adds fifteen new Callings to the original ten. Each has Level Features at 1/3/6/9/12/15/18 instead of "paths" — Specialities are taken as the character grows. From the GM's chair, the practical changes are:

  • Calling Features are baseline. A Legionary's Shield Doctrine always works; the player doesn't trade for it. Build encounters expecting these to be in play.
  • Specialities are choosing-moments. When a player gains a Speciality, ask them to pick Advantage or +4. They cannot have both. Specialities never stack.
  • Multicalling caps at Lv 6 of the Secondary. Don't let players "buy" the Lv 18 capstone of two Callings. The hard limit is two Callings, never more.

Social and Magical Callings express Pressure rather than damage. They make encounters shorter rather than easier — enemies hesitate, miss turns, surrender, or break composure before they break their bones.

Running the Five Magic Systems

Each tradition has a distinct economy. Treat them as a system of trade-offs, not flavour reskins.

Mana — Predictable, Recoverable, Limited

Mana magic is the easiest to balance: it has a pool, a cost, and known recovery curves. Build encounters around spell economy: how many T2-T3 spells can the Arcanist afford before they're empty?

Backlash matters. When a player rolls Dire Failure on a Mana cast, choose or roll d6: Burn (1d6 EP, unreduced), Sear (Overstrained), Rift-Sense (glowing, sparking — Stealth ruined for the scene), Slip (lose 1 AP, Off-Balance), Fray (Drained — MP max -10% until Long Rest), or Wrong Attention (omen, watcher, complication). The last one is your gift — use it. A failed casting is a hook.

Overcasting is for legends and corpses. Allow it, but ensure consequences (EP burn cannot be reduced; failed Mana Save means Backlash; Dire Failure on overcast = worse Backlash and possibly Corruption check).

Blood — Honest, Visible, Punishing

Blood magic spends EP and accepts conditions. It's powerful because it always works — there's no fizzle on a failed casting, only consequence. Lean into this:

  • Blood-healing is the best healing in the game. It is also the most costly. A Hemomancer keeping a party alive should be visibly hurting.
  • Civilised settings react. -2 Standing for visible blood casting is automatic. Watchmen take note. Temples disapprove. Don't let the player forget.
  • External blood is the gateway to Corruption. Save Will saves vs DC 12+Tier. Track Marks separately from Soul Taint or Void Corruption — same word, different meters.

Divine — Conditional, Social, Theological

Divine magic does not misfire. It refuses. A player who breaks Oaths loses access, full stop. Be a fair judge — Minor Oath -1 Favour temporarily, Major Oath -2 and require atonement, Sacred Oath immediate Forsaken state.

Atonement is a session, not a sentence. Confession, restitution, rite, sacrifice — any of these. Make atonement a quest, not a check-box. The player should feel the cost narratively.

Favour above +3 is generous — let it happen, but reward gods accordingly. Chosen vessels (Favour +5-6) should attract divine notice and divine enemies.

Soul — Surgical, Permanent, Quiet

Soul magic is hardest to adjudicate because the costs are non-numeric. Use Soul Integrity as your anchor — SI loss is permanent unless treated, so a Soulbinder who burns SI casually will visibly degrade.

Soul Echoes are roleplay hooks. When a player gains one, ask them: "Whose voice do you hear?" "What memory is not yours?" Let it inform future scenes. An Echo that doesn't matter is a missed opportunity.

Bound souls are a legal and moral crisis. If a player carries a soul token, the world should notice — soul-wardens, priests, exorcists. Soul magic is restricted precisely because it works.

Void — Campaign-Level, Always Costly

Void magic is not for spam. The Absolute Rule (one Void working per scene, or accept Dire Failure) exists to keep this true. Enforce it.

Track Attention as a GM resource. Each Void working raises it. At thresholds you choose, things happen: dreams worsen, anomalies appear, entities observe, an avatar arrives. Don't show the meter to players. They should feel it building.

Open Void use is a campaign event. Faction reaction is automatic — Standing collapses, Oaths invalidate, divine hostility, soul-warden contracts. If a player uses Void in public, half a session of consequences is the minimum.

Running Pressure in Combat

Pressure (Social) and Arcane Pressure (Magical) are how non-damage Callings stay relevant in a fight. They impose Suppressed / Shaken / Frightened on enemies who fail Will saves. The system is reliable; the question is when to let it apply.

Who is Affected?

  • Always affected: trained soldiers, guards, citizens, structured enemies that recognise authority.
  • Sometimes affected: bandits, mercenaries — they may resist if the social Calling has no Standing leverage over them.
  • Usually immune: beasts, mindless undead, demons. The body still hesitates (Suppressed), but Shaken and Frightened often do not stick.
  • Inverted: cultists and zealots. Divine Pressure from a heretic may energise them. Be ready to invert effects narratively.

Calling-Specific Notes

Envoy / Magistrate. Pressure scales with Standing in the local jurisdiction. A Magistrate in their own city is terrifying; in foreign lands they're just a person with paperwork.

Spymaster. Pressure works best in private. In public spotlight, the false face costs them their edge.

Arcanist. Substitutes Wit for Corruption contribution. They pressure by certainty, not rot.

Hemomancer. APrV +1 while Wounded. Blood smell. Visible suffering as authority.

Ordained. Imposes Disadvantage on corrupted, undead, and profane saves. Use this. Run undead encounters with Ordained PCs as fast — the divine should feel real.

Soulbinder. Disadvantage on possessing spirits. They are the answer to demon-haunted dungeons.

Void-Touched. Partial Success or worse always introduces a minor complication. Reality reacts to them constantly. Lean in: scenery, dreams, animal behaviour. The world is uncomfortable.

Pacing Pressure

Pressure is checked once per turn, not stacked across multiple sources in the same round. If two PCs both project Pressure, pick the higher PV and apply only that check. Otherwise initiative grinds to a halt.

Each social character may cause only three major Social Effects per scene (Rally, Expose, Command, Demand Surrender). Pressure aura does not count toward this limit. This keeps social dominance powerful without becoming oppressive.

Adjudicating Standing & Reputation

Standing (-2 to +6) and Reputation (-5 to +5 per faction) are the two social meters. From the GM's side:

When Standing Applies

Yes: if the scene is about authority — courts, guards, customs, audiences, official transactions. Add Standing to the roll. Composure = 10 + Bearing/Will + Standing.

No: if the scene is about friendship, intimacy, family, or trust. A high-Standing noble has no inherent advantage in winning a friend's affection.

When Reputation Applies

Reputation modifies social rolls with the faction that knows you. d20 + Ability + Training + Reputation. A character with +3 Reputation with the Phoenix Agency gains +3 on rolls dealing with their operatives, archives, or safehouses — and only then.

Shifting Reputation

  • Minor deed: ±1
  • Notable deed: ±2
  • Great deed: ±3
  • Legendary deed: ±4 or more (rare)

Shifts may be delayed until news travels. A great deed in a distant province may take a season to register with central factions. This is a narrative resource — use it.

Disgraced

A character becomes Disgraced when publicly shamed, convicted, or proven an oath-breaker. -2 Standing in that realm and -2 on Bearing-based rolls with anyone who knows. Removal requires pardon, restitution, heroic deed, or sacred absolution. Disgraced is a story state. Treat its removal as worth a session of effort, minimum.

Inter-Calling Balance Law

The synergy actions are powerful by design. If a single combo (Pin → Finish forever, Shield & Spear every round) becomes the default loop, two responses:

  1. Increase enemy counterplay. Shields, smoke, mass, terrain that breaks line of sight, multiple targets, ranged threat that punishes positioning.
  2. Limit the combo. Cap to once per round or once per scene. This is a legitimate ruling.

The principle: if a rule stops creating decisions, it stops being a rule and starts being a habit. Break habits.
